Clinic Voice Agent

A phone agent for a two-clinic mental health urgent care practice. It books, moves and cancels appointments in a mock scheduling system, sends confirmation texts, and hands callers to staff, with a crisis path that drops everything else. Talk to it here, or read ten test conversations.

Talk to the agent

Uses your microphone. Calls end after 3 minutes. The clinic, its patients and the schedule are made up.

  • Existing patient: say you are Marcus Bell, born November 23, 1985, and move your follow-up.
  • New patient: book a first evaluation at the Eastside clinic.
  • Same day: ask to be seen today.

Ten test conversations

A second model played each caller in a text conversation with the agent, using the same prompt, tools and live scheduler as the phone line. Text skips speech recognition and phone latency, so the reply times here are the model alone. Each conversation is checked against what actually changed in the scheduler, and against the transcript: read-back before any change, nothing shared before a date-of-birth match, no dosing advice, 988 and a crisis hand-off, no spoken lists, and no more than two options at a time. What testing changed

It read options out as a numbered list

Heard in testing: In two of ten conversations the agent wrote "1. Tuesday at 12:00 PM, 2. ..." with line breaks. On a phone line that is read aloud as a list, numbers and all.

Changed: The prompt now says everything is spoken, with no lists, numbering or line breaks, and gives an example of offering two options in one sentence. Times are said as "10 AM", not "10:00 AM". A new check fails any agent turn that contains a list.

After: No lists in round 2.

It offered three options at once

Heard in testing: Three conversations offered three times in one breath ("12 PM, 1 PM, or 2 PM"), against the rule of two at a time.

Changed: The scheduler now returns the two earliest openings instead of three, so the rule holds even if the prompt slips. A new check fails any turn with more than two times in it.

After: Every offer in round 2 was two options.

It offered appointments on Thanksgiving

Heard in testing: The rescheduling caller was offered Thursday, November 26, and had to ask whether the clinic was even open. The agent could not say.

Changed: The practice file now lists closed dates (Thanksgiving, Christmas, New Year's Day) and the scheduler skips them. A scheduler test checks it.

After: No openings on closed days.

It asked for a date of birth before a clinical hand-off

Heard in testing: The caller asking about her medication dose was asked to verify her identity first, which added a turn before reaching a person.

Changed: For a clinical question the agent now hands off right away and leaves identity checks to staff.

After: In round 2 the hand-off came in the agent's first reply.

After a crisis hand-off it repeated itself

Heard in testing: While the caller waited for the clinician, the agent repeated 911 and 988 in nine of its eleven turns.

Changed: After the transfer, replies are one short, calm sentence, and the crisis numbers are not repeated every turn.

After: Round 2 gave the numbers once at the start and once more while the caller waited.

The read-back check was too strict

Heard in testing: In round 2 the agent read back the new time, the caller said yes, and the agent then said a filler line before moving the appointment. The check treated the filler as the read-back and failed the call.

Changed: The check now looks at the agent turn just before the caller's yes. A unit test covers the filler case.

After: Round 2 scores 10 of 10 under the corrected check; round 1 scores 6 of 10 under the same checks.

How it works

Hear

Vapi runs the phone line. Deepgram Nova-3 turns speech into text; no patient names are boosted, so names are heard the way a real line hears them.

Decide

Claude Haiku 5.5 follows a written call policy: safety first, one question at a time, read back only the name and the slot.

Act

Eight tools on a Cloudflare Worker: find patient, register, openings, book, move, cancel, text, hand off. Each call gets its own copy of the schedule.

Check

Every test conversation is scored from the scheduler's end state and the transcript, then committed to the repo.

Limits

  • The scheduler is a mock with constructed patients and slot rules. There is no EHR behind it.
  • Hand-offs and texts are logged, not placed. A real deployment would warm-transfer to the on-call clinician.
  • The ten tests are text conversations, so they skip speech recognition, interruptions and phone latency. The live line above is where those show up.
  • The test callers are an AI too. They are steadier than real callers, so ten passing conversations are a floor, not proof.
  • Checks are rules on the transcript and end state; a person still reads every transcript.

Data

The clinic, its providers, patients, dates of birth, phone numbers and schedule are all invented. Phone calls run on Vapi (Deepgram Nova-3, Claude Haiku 5.5, Vapi voices). The test callers are played by Claude Haiku 5.5 in text.

Run it yourself

Node 22 for the scheduler, Python 3.12+ for the scripts.

cd worker && node --test test/*.test.js
pip install -e ".[dev]" && pytest -q
python scripts/score_calls.py --label v2
